# Privacy Policy for Wordminator

**Last updated:** 26 October 2026

Wordminator is a free word game with four minigames in five languages, developed by broth3rsdev. This policy explains what happens to your data when you play it.

## Summary

There is no account and no sign-in. Wordminator has no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no tracking SDKs. I run no servers and never receive your personal data. Everything the game remembers about you - your scores, achievements, streaks and settings - stays on your device.

The game goes online in exactly one case: when you ask for the definition of a word that the bundled dictionary does not define, it looks the word up on Wiktionary. That request goes directly from your device to the Wikimedia Foundation, exactly as if you had opened the page yourself. Nothing else ever leaves your device.

## Information stored on your device

The game stores the following locally, in its private app storage:

- Your play history: for each game you finish, the minigame, the dictionary language, the difficulty, the score, whether you won, how long it took, when you played, and the round's details, such as the hidden word and the words you found or guessed.
- Your achievements, play and win streaks, daily challenge results, and the words you have discovered across all games.
- Your settings: UI language, music and sound volumes, haptics, the theme, and the last dictionary language, diacritics choice and difficulty you picked for each game.
- The dictionaries themselves, unpacked from the app on first launch, plus any definitions fetched from Wiktionary, kept so the same word opens offline next time.

None of this is transmitted to me. The game opts out of Android's automatic backup, so nothing is copied to a cloud backup either. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in system settings, removes all of the above.

## Information sent to third parties

Using the game causes your device to contact these services directly:

- **Wiktionary**, run by the Wikimedia Foundation (`en.wiktionary.org`, `de.wiktionary.org`, `es.wiktionary.org`, `fr.wiktionary.org`, `ro.wiktionary.org`). When you tap a word for its definition and the bundled dictionary has none, the game requests that word's page from the Wiktionary edition of the dictionary language you are playing in. The request carries the word and reaches Wikimedia with your IP address. No scores, settings or device identifiers are sent. This is the only reason the game asks for internet access. See [foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy).
- **Google Play**, only if you tap "Drop Us a Review!", which opens the game's store listing. Google Play also collects installation and diagnostic data as store operator, independently of this game. See [policies.google.com/privacy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

That is the complete list. The game loads no remote content, checks no server on launch, and talks to no one else.

## Analytics, crash reporting and advertising

There are none. The game contains no analytics, telemetry, attribution, or advertising SDKs, and no crash reports are transmitted anywhere. Diagnostic logs exist only in memory while the game runs.

If you use the feedback link in Settings, your email app opens with my address and a subject line filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself, and you can edit or delete anything beforehand.

## Cookies

The game sets no cookies and keeps no login session. The Wiktionary lookup is a plain read of a public page.

## Permissions

- **Internet:** only to fetch a definition from Wiktionary when the bundled dictionary has none.
- **Vibration:** for the haptic tap you feel on buttons and tiles, which you can switch off in Settings.

The game requests no location, contacts, microphone, camera, storage, or photo access.

## Legal basis and your rights (EEA/UK)

Where the GDPR applies, the only processing the game carries out beyond your device - the Wiktionary lookup - happens at your request, when you tap a word, and rests on legitimate interest in showing you the definition you asked for. There is no account and I hold no copy of your data, so requests for access, correction, erasure, portability, or objection are satisfied on your device by clearing the game's storage or uninstalling it. Requests concerning data held by the Wikimedia Foundation or Google should be directed to them. You may lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority.

## Sale of personal information

I do not sell or share your personal information, and I never have. This includes "sale" and "sharing" as defined by the California Consumer Privacy Act.

## Data retention and deletion

I retain nothing, because I receive nothing. Locally stored data is kept until you clear the game's storage or uninstall it.

## Children's privacy

Wordminator is a word game suitable for all ages. It collects no personal information from anyone, children included, and has no chat, no accounts, no advertising and no purchases. If you are a parent or guardian with a concern, contact me at the address below.

## Security

Data stored by the game lives in its private storage area, protected by your device's operating system and, where enabled, device encryption. The Wiktionary lookup uses HTTPS. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, so absolute security cannot be guaranteed.

## Changes to this policy

I may update this policy as the game changes, for example if an online multiplayer mode is added. The revised version will be posted on this page with a new "Last updated" date, and takes effect when posted.
